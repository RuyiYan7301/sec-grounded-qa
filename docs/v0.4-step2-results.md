# v0.4 step 2 results: a 1.7B planner (SFT, GRPO, constrained decoding) against the 8B planner

Protocol: `docs/v0.4-plan.md` sections 3, 3b, 3c, 3d and their dated amendments; the test procedure
amendment (2026-11-19) was committed before any arm was run on a test set. Runner
`evaluation/v4/run_step2.py`, tables `evaluation/v4/summarize_step2.py`; outputs in `results/v0.4/step2/`.
Only the planner differs between arms: the executor, the `qwen3:8b` renderer and the frozen scorer 2.1
are the same for all.

Test sets: **t1** = the frozen v0.2 Part A test (159 items after one exclusion: 80 generated, 40
handwritten, 39 adversarial; 6 companies); **t2** = 160 generated questions on 15 held-out companies,
held-out templates only. Engine and gold are pre-2c (amendment); gold plans execute correctly on 159/159
and 160/160.

## Arms

| arm | model | prompt | decoding |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 | Qwen3-1.7B base | full v0.4 planner prompt | free |
| B1 | Qwen3-1.7B base | full | constrained (xgrammar, plan schema) |
| S | SFT checkpoint-480 | short + company hint | constrained |
| Sfree | SFT checkpoint-480 | short + hint | free |
| Slong | SFT checkpoint-480 | full | constrained |
| R | SFT + GRPO checkpoint-150 | short + hint | constrained |
| T | `qwen3:8b` (Ollama), frozen v0.2 settings | full | constrained (Ollama `format`) |

All 1.7B arms: greedy, thinking off, one repair call. Planned comparisons: B0-B1, B1-S, S-R, R-T.

### Where the differences come from (planner or renderer)

The renderer is the same 8B model for all arms but is not deterministic across calls, and its input
includes the plan. Each discordant item was split by whether the two arms' *execution* correctness also
differs (planner side) or not (renderer side):

| pair | final discordant | planner side | renderer side |
|---|---|---|---|
| t1 S vs R | 0 / 4 | 0 / 3 | 0 / 1 |
| t1 R vs T | 8 / 2 | 2 / 2 | 6 / 0 |
| t2 R vs T | 6 / 0 | 5 / 0 | 1 / 0 |
| t2 S vs Sfree (identical plans) | 0 / 2 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 2 |

- **t2, S vs Sfree vs R**: all three produce the same 160 plans; final correct is 150, 152, 150. These two
  items are renderer variation alone, a floor for reading small gaps.
- **t1 R vs T**: on execution correctness T is slightly ahead (5 / 3). R's final-correct lead comes from 6
  renderer-side items, 5 of them refusals where both arms' execution has the same outcome; R's plans carry
  the fixed refusal reasons learned in SFT ("Investment advice, forecasts and opinions are not answered from
  filings."), which the renderer reuses and the frozen refusal check accepts, while T's free-text reasons
  lead to answers the check rejects. This is a real effect of the plan text on the rendered answer, not a
  better plan.
- **t2 R vs T**: 5 of 6 are planner side and are one failure mode: T writes `periods: ["FY2023",
  "FY2023"]` on restated-value questions, which the schema forbids (`uniqueItems`); the repair call repeats
  it. This is the failure already seen in 2b (2/12 there; 5/12 here, all from the single held-out
  restated-value template). Ollama's `format` constraint does not enforce `uniqueItems`.
- **t1 S vs R**: 3 of R's 4 gains are handwritten questions S refused as out_of_ontology (stock-based
  compensation, free cash flow, two original-vs-current questions); R planned them and they are correct.

## What the results support

1. **SFT is the step that matters.** B1 -> S: +72 (t1) and +76 (t2) items, p < 1e-18 on both.
2. **Constrained decoding helps the base model, not the trained one.** B0 -> B1 raises valid plans
   (119 -> 158, 125 -> 160) and is significant on t2 (+16 net) but not on t1 (+5 net); B1 still has many
   wrong answers (silent wrong 60 and 67), because valid JSON is not a correct plan. On the SFT model it
   changes nothing measurable (S vs Sfree: identical plans on t2, one plan difference on t1).
3. **GRPO adds nothing detectable on these test sets.** S vs R: 0 / 4 on t1 (p 0.33 after Holm), identical
   plans on t2. Its gain on `val_hard` (344 -> 375) was on the hard-wording style it trained on; the test
   sets do not contain that style, and t2 is at ceiling for both (160/160 execution correct). Silent wrong is
   the same for S and R on both test sets (8 and 10).
4. **The 1.7B planner matches the 8B planner here; it does not beat it on planning.** R vs T: final correct
   150 vs 144 (t1) and 150 vs 144 (t2), neither significant after Holm (0.33, 0.063). On t1 T is slightly
   ahead on execution correctness; R's final-correct lead there is refusal wording. On t2 R's lead is one
   T failure mode on one template.
5. **The SFT model needs its training prompt.** With the full planner prompt (Slong) it falls to 110 and
   104, with 45 and 55 silent wrong answers.
6. **Cost.** 1.7B: 3.5 GB GPU peak, 0.21 s per plan in batches of 16, 3.3 s per plan at batch 1 (t2, arm R,
   same 160 plans). 8B through Ollama: 2.5 s per plan, one question at a time. At batch 1 the 1.7B planner
   (HF transformers + xgrammar on Windows) is not faster than the 8B planner (llama.cpp); the latency gain
   exists only when plans are batched.

Claim this supports: "A Qwen3-1.7B planner trained by SFT on generated plans reaches the 8B planner's final
correct on both test sets (150 vs 144, 150 vs 144; differences not significant), with 3.5 GB of GPU memory;
GRPO on hard wordings did not change test results."

## Limits

- One run per arm; the renderer alone moves final correct by up to 2 items on identical plans.
- t2 is at ceiling for S, Sfree and R on execution, so it cannot separate them.
- Pre-2c engine and gold (fiscal-year labels of HD, LOW, JNJ; operating-expense tags): not measured here.
- Test set 1's companies were seen in v0.2 development; its handwritten and adversarial items are the
  unseen-wording part, 79 items.
